# Photos — how to swap in real images

Right now every image slot uses **our own illustrations** (in `assets/img/art/`). They're drawn from scratch for PawHomie, so there's no licence, attribution or takedown risk.

When you want real photos, you don't need to touch any code.

---

## How to add a photo

Save the image into **`assets/img/photos/`** using the exact filename below. That's it — the app uses the photo if it's there and falls back to the illustration if it isn't.

| Filename | Where it appears | Good size |
|---|---|---|
| `hero.jpg` | Landing page, big image beside the headline | 1200 × 820 |
| `cover.jpg` | Top of a Paw Homie's profile | 1200 × 620 |
| `home.jpg` | "Home & walk area" — the house/yard | 800 × 520 |
| `walk.jpg` | "Home & walk area" — the walking route | 800 × 520 |
| `pet.jpg` | Pet profile page | 900 × 540 |

Use `.jpg`, keep each file **under ~400 KB** (large photos make the site feel slow on phones). [squoosh.app](https://squoosh.app) compresses them for free.

---

## What to search for

On **unsplash.com** or **pexels.com**:

- **hero** — "woman with dog at home", "dog and cat together", "happy dog living room". Want: warm, bright, homey, natural light. Avoid: studio white backgrounds, anything cold or clinical.
- **cover** — "person petting dog", "dog on sofa", "cat on lap". Should feel like *someone's actual home*, not a photoshoot.
- **home** — "house exterior with garden", "fenced backyard", "cozy living room".
- **walk** — "park path trees", "dog walking in park", "neighbourhood sidewalk trees".
- **pet** — "beagle portrait", "dog looking at camera", "cat close up".

Pick photos with **similar warm tones** so they sit well next to the teal/gold/cream brand. A quick tell: if a photo looks good next to the logo, it works.

---

## ⚠️ Two licence things worth knowing

**1. Free doesn't mean unrestricted.** Unsplash and Pexels licences allow commercial use without attribution, but both prohibit *reselling the photo itself* and using photos to suggest endorsement. Fine for a marketplace site. Always download from the official site, never from Google Images — image search results are frequently copyrighted.

**2. Photos of people are the risky ones.** A stock photo of a person usually has **no model release**, and PawHomie is a commercial service. Using a stranger's face next to text like *"Sara M., verified Paw Homie"* implies they're a real user of your business — that's the kind of thing that draws complaints, and in Canada it can raise personality/publicity-rights issues.

**Safer approach:**
- For **hero/cover**, prefer photos where the person's **face isn't identifiable** (from behind, cropped, hands only) — or just use pets alone.
- For anything that looks like a **specific named sitter**, use real photos of real sitters once you have them, or keep the illustration.
- If Bilal wants people's faces for launch, buy from a paid library with a model release (Adobe Stock, iStock) — usually $10–30 an image.

This isn't a reason to avoid stock photos. It's a reason to keep faces out of the "this is one of our sitters" slots until you have real ones.

---

## Suggested order

1. **`home.jpg` and `walk.jpg` first** — these are pets/places, zero people-risk, and they appear on every sitter profile.
2. **`pet.jpg`** — easy, just an animal.
3. **`hero.jpg` and `cover.jpg` last** — these are the ones where the model-release question matters, so they deserve a moment's thought.
